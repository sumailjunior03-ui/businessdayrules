Payroll Timing Reference

When Does My Paycheck Actually Arrive?

Your paycheck's arrival depends on payroll submission, ACH processing, and your bank's posting schedule.

Home / Paycheck Timing

Your paycheck's arrival depends on when your employer submits payroll, how long ACH processing takes, and when your bank posts funds — delays at any step can push your pay back by one to several calendar days.

What actually happens after payroll is submitted

Payroll doesn't move instantly. Here's the full chain:

  1. Employer submits payroll — Your employer sends payroll data to a payroll processor (ADP, Gusto, QuickBooks, etc.) with a scheduled pay date.
  2. Payroll processor batches the submission — The processor groups all employee payments and submits them to the ACH network, usually 1–2 business days before the pay date.
  3. ACH network processes the transfer — The Federal Reserve's ACH system settles the transaction on the next business day (or same day if submitted before the same-day ACH window).
  4. Your bank receives the funds — The bank gets the settlement file from the ACH network.
  5. Your bank posts the funds — The bank makes the money available in your account. This is when you actually see the deposit.

Steps 2 through 4 each take at least one business day. A late submission, a weekend, or a holiday at any step delays everything downstream.

What happens based on when payroll is submitted

Scenario A — Submitted on time

Situation: Your employer submitted payroll on schedule, 1–2 business days before your pay date.

Cause: The payroll processor had time to batch and submit to ACH before the cutoff.

Arrival estimate: Funds post on your scheduled pay date, typically between midnight and 9:00 AM. You are on track — no delay.

Scenario B — Submitted after cutoff

Situation: Your employer submitted payroll after the payroll processor's cutoff time.

Cause: The submission missed the current batch. It rolls to the next business day for processing.

Arrival estimate: Your paycheck arrives one business day late. If pay date was Friday and payroll was submitted late, funds post Monday.

Scenario C — Submitted before weekend

Situation: Payroll was submitted Friday, with a Monday or Tuesday pay date.

Cause: ACH does not process on weekends. Friday submissions that miss same-day windows begin processing Monday.

Arrival estimate: If submitted before Friday's cutoff, funds post Monday. If submitted after Friday's cutoff, funds post Monday or Tuesday.

Scenario D — Submitted before holiday

Situation: Payroll was submitted on or before a federal holiday.

Cause: The Federal Reserve does not settle ACH on federal holidays. Processing pauses until the next business day.

Arrival estimate: Add one business day for each holiday in the chain. A Monday holiday after a Friday submission means processing starts Tuesday and funds arrive Tuesday or Wednesday.

Scenario E — Early pay vs standard posting

Situation: Your coworker received their paycheck before you did, even though you have the same employer and pay date.

Cause: Different banks post funds at different times.

Arrival estimate: Early-pay banks may post 1–2 days before the official pay date. Standard banks post on the pay date itself.

Why your paycheck feels "late"

There are three moments in the payroll chain, and they are not the same thing:

  • Submission — Your employer sends payroll data to the processor.
  • Processing — The ACH network settles the transaction between banks.
  • Posting — Your bank makes the funds available in your account.

Your employer may have submitted on time, but processing and posting each take a business day. A weekend or holiday between any of these steps adds calendar days with zero processing progress.

The system didn't delay your paycheck — it hasn't started processing it yet.

Why some people get paid early

Some banks — including Chime, Varo, Current, and Wells Fargo's Early Pay Day — make direct deposits available before the official settlement date. They advance the funds to you based on the payment file they receive, before the ACH network has actually settled the transaction.

This means two employees at the same company, with the same pay date, can receive their money on different days. One bank posts on Wednesday for a Friday payday. Another waits until Friday morning.

This is a bank choice, not a payroll choice. Your employer did not send the money at different times. Your bank decided when to make it available. Early posting is not guaranteed — banks can change the timing or stop offering it at any time.

Common questions

When does direct deposit usually hit?

Most direct deposits post between midnight and 9:00 AM on your scheduled pay date. The exact time depends on your bank's posting schedule.

What time of day do paychecks post?

Most major banks post deposits at the start of the business day. Some online banks post as early as the evening before the pay date.

What if my paycheck is late?

First, check whether your employer submitted payroll on time. If they did, the delay is likely from ACH processing or your bank's posting schedule. Contact your employer's payroll department to confirm the submission date.

Do weekends delay payroll deposits?

Yes. ACH does not process on weekends. If payroll is submitted Friday after cutoff, processing begins Monday. Funds post Monday or Tuesday.

What happens if payroll is submitted late?

Late submission pushes the entire chain back by at least one business day. Your paycheck will arrive one business day after the originally scheduled pay date, or later if a weekend or holiday falls in between.

Why did my coworker get paid before me?

Different banks post direct deposits at different times. Some banks offer early direct deposit and make funds available 1–2 days before the pay date. If you and your coworker use different banks, your posting times will differ even though your employer sent the same payment at the same time.
